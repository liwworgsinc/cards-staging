How NFC fits

The NFC item is the shortcut. Your digital card is the experience.

NFC does not replace your digital business card. A compatible NFC chip stores a small instruction—usually a URL—that can open your LIW Card when tapped against a supported phone.

NFC business card vs. QR business card

Both methods can open the same digital business card. NFC is useful for a quick tap in person, while QR is easy to display on a phone screen, sign, flyer, table display or printed card. A direct link works well in text messages, email signatures and social profiles.

Using all three gives you flexibility. You are not forcing every customer to use the same technology, and you do not have to rebuild your online card when you change the way you share it.

What should I put on an NFC business card?

The strongest setup is usually not a long list of raw contact details stored directly on the NFC chip. Instead, point the NFC item to your live digital card. That keeps the chip simple and lets you update your online profile later without reprogramming every customer interaction.

  • Phone, text and email actions
  • Website and social profiles
  • Contact saving
  • Services, products or content on eligible plans
  • Booking, payment or other supported external links
Questions

NFC business card FAQ

Does LIW Cards require NFC?

No. Your LIW Card works as a digital business card without NFC. You can share it by QR code or direct link.

Can an NFC tag open my LIW Card?

Yes, if you use a compatible programmable NFC item and write your public LIW Card URL to it. Device compatibility and NFC behavior can vary.

Do customers need an LIW Cards app?

No. Your public card opens in the browser, so the person receiving it does not need to install an LIW Cards app.

Is QR better than NFC?

Neither is always better. NFC can feel faster in person, while QR works well on screens and printed materials. Using both gives you more ways to share.
